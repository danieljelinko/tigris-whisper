Monday, 15 July

The train pulled into Keleti station at twenty-five past nine, almost exactly on schedule despite the short delay at the border. After the long ride from Vienna, it felt good to step onto the platform and stretch my legs. I found a taxi outside the main hall and asked the driver to take me to the hotel on Andrássy Avenue, a journey of about fifteen minutes through the morning traffic.

Check-in wasn't until two in the afternoon, so I left my suitcase with the front desk and went out to explore the neighborhood. The avenue was lined with grand nineteenth-century buildings, many of them recently restored, and I counted at least six embassies within the first ten minutes of walking. Around noon, I stopped at a small café for a coffee and a slice of strudel, which cost about one thousand two hundred forints.

By early afternoon my room was ready on the fourth floor, with a narrow balcony overlooking a quiet courtyard. I unpacked quickly and rested for an hour before heading down to the Danube for an evening walk. The river was busy with sightseeing boats, and the lights along both banks were just beginning to switch on as the sun went down around eight. I crossed the Chain Bridge on foot, pausing halfway to take photographs of the Parliament building, which was lit up in gold against the darkening sky. For dinner, I found a small restaurant near Vörösmarty Square and ordered goulash soup followed by stuffed cabbage, all for under five thousand forints including a glass of red wine.

Tuesday, 16 July

I woke up at seven and had breakfast on the hotel terrace: scrambled eggs, fresh bread, and a pot of strong coffee. The plan for the day was to explore Buda, on the other side of the river, so I took the metro two stops and then walked up toward the castle district. The funicular up to Buda Castle was running every few minutes, and the ride itself took less than two minutes, climbing steeply above the rooftops with a wonderful view back toward Pest.

At the top, I spent almost two hours wandering through the castle grounds and the surrounding streets, eventually reaching Fisherman's Bastion just before eleven. The seven white towers are said to represent the seven Magyar tribes that settled the Carpathian Basin more than a thousand years ago, and from the terraces the view over the Danube and the Parliament building was even more impressive than from the bridge the night before. I bought a ticket for the upper terrace, which cost around eight hundred forints, and stayed there for almost half an hour just looking at the city.

For lunch I found a small bistro on Castle Hill and had a chicken paprikash with dumplings. In the afternoon I walked back down toward the river and crossed back to Pest using the Elisabeth Bridge this time, a white suspension bridge a short distance south of the Chain Bridge. By six in the evening I was back near the hotel, tired but happy, and decided to book a one-hour river cruise for that night. The boat left from a dock near the Chain Bridge at half past eight and gave a completely different perspective on the city, with both Buda Castle and the Parliament building illuminated and reflected in the water.

Wednesday, 17 July

This was meant to be a quieter day. I went for an early walk on Margaret Island, which sits in the middle of the Danube and is closed to regular traffic, so it was wonderfully peaceful at seven in the morning. I walked almost the full length of the island, about two and a half kilometers, past the musical fountain, the rose garden, and the ruins of an old monastery, before stopping for coffee at a small kiosk near the water tower.

Back on the Pest side by ten, I headed to the Great Market Hall, a huge nineteenth-century building with three floors of stalls selling everything from paprika and sausages to lace tablecloths and souvenirs. I bought a small bag of paprika to take home, along with a jar of honey, for around two thousand five hundred forints altogether. For lunch I tried lángos, a fried dough topped with sour cream and cheese, from one of the stalls upstairs.

In the afternoon I took the metro out to Heroes' Square, with its tall central column and statues of the seven chieftains, and then walked into City Park behind it. The park was busy with families, joggers, and a few street performers near the entrance to Vajdahunyad Castle. I finished the day at the Széchenyi thermal baths, which are right next to the park entrance. I spent almost three hours there, alternating between the outdoor pools, which were warm even though the air temperature had dropped, and one of the indoor saunas. It was, without question, the highlight of the whole trip.

Thursday, 18 July

My train back to Vienna was scheduled to leave at eleven in the morning, so I had time for one last coffee near the hotel before checking out at ten. At Keleti station, however, an announcement informed passengers that the eleven o'clock service was delayed by around forty minutes due to a signaling problem further along the line. I used the extra time to buy a few last postcards from a kiosk on the platform.

The train eventually departed at twenty to twelve, almost exactly forty minutes late as announced, and the journey back passed quickly. I spent most of it writing up these notes and looking out at the same fields of sunflowers I had seen on the way in, three days earlier. Despite the delay, I reached Vienna only about half an hour later than originally planned, with plenty of time to catch my connecting train home.
