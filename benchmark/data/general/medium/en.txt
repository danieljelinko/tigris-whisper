The train departed from Vienna at seven fifteen, right on schedule, and rolled smoothly through the countryside toward Budapest. Outside the window, fields of sunflowers stretched toward the horizon, broken occasionally by small villages with red-tiled roofs. Around nine o'clock, an announcement informed passengers that the dining car was open for breakfast, so I walked down two carriages and ordered coffee with a croissant. By half past nine, the train crossed the border, and a conductor checked everyone's passports. We arrived at Keleti station almost exactly on time, just before half past nine local time, after the one-hour time difference.
